Chapter one

The flower

Before her knee replacement, Mrs. P. had stopped stepping out of her home altogether.

On paper, my job after the surgery was her knee. My real job was the front door. So we went downstairs for a walk, together.

On one of those walks, I gave her a flower.

She is 75 now. She doesn't walk anymore. She sprints.

Chapter two

Orange juice

Not every session ends on a treatment table.

One morning, it ended at breakfast with my client. Two glasses of orange juice, and a cheers.

That's the part of recovery nobody writes in the notes: getting your ordinary mornings back.

What it means to be a physiotherapist

It means I get to be there for the first step, the bad day, and the day you no longer need me.

What I do differently

Three things you'll notice.

  1. 01

    I start with your life, not your scan.

    Tell me what you miss doing. That becomes the plan.

  2. 02

    I show up as a person.

    Flowers, breakfasts, the occasional bad joke. Trust does more than any machine.

  3. 03

    We celebrate the small wins.

    The first stair. The first walk outside. They all count, and they all get a cheers.
